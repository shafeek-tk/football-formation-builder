9-a-side Formations Explained

9-a-side is the bridge to the full game for many youth players. Eight outfield players allow back threes and back fours, so it is a good format to learn proper tactics.

Open the free 9-a-side lineup builder

3-3-2

Three defenders, three midfielders and two forwards.

Strengths
Balanced across every line with a strike partnership.
Watch out for
Wide areas can be exposed if the outside midfielders push on.
Best for
Most teams; the most common 9v9 shape.

3-2-3

Three defenders, two midfielders and a front three.

Strengths
Wide attackers give width and lots of crosses.
Watch out for
Two in midfield can be overrun.
Best for
Teams with quick wide players.
Use 3-2-3 in the lineup builder →

4-3-1

A back four, three midfielders and a striker.

Strengths
Very solid and mirrors the 11-a-side 4-3-3 shape.
Watch out for
The lone striker needs midfield support.
Best for
Teams preparing for 11-a-side.
Use 4-3-1 in the lineup builder →

4-2-2

A back four, two midfielders and two forwards.

Strengths
Familiar shape, simple roles, two strikers.
Watch out for
Midfield pair has a lot of ground to cover.
Best for
Direct teams with a good front two.
Use 4-2-2 in the lineup builder →

3-4-1

Three defenders, four midfielders and a striker.

Strengths
Dominates midfield and keeps the ball.
Watch out for
Needs fit wide midfielders to support attack and defence.
Best for
Possession teams.
