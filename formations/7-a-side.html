7-a-side Formations Explained

7-a-side is the standard format for youth football and a favourite for Sunday leagues. Six outfield players give you enough to build proper lines, and the 2-3-1 is the most used shape by far.

Open the free 7-a-side lineup builder

2-3-1

Two defenders, three midfielders and a striker. The most popular 7-a-side shape.

Strengths
Balanced, strong in midfield, easy for young players to learn.
Watch out for
Wide midfielders need the legs to help the two defenders.
Best for
Almost any team; the best starting point.

3-2-1

Three at the back, two midfielders and a striker.

Strengths
Very secure defensively with cover across the pitch.
Watch out for
Can feel short of attacking players.
Best for
Facing strong attackers or defending a lead.

1-3-2

A sweeper, three midfielders and two strikers.

Strengths
Attacks with numbers and presses high.
Watch out for
One defender leaves big gaps on the counter.
Best for
Dominant teams that keep the ball.

2-2-2

Two defenders, two midfielders, two forwards.

Strengths
Simple and symmetrical, everyone has a partner.
Watch out for
Can be outnumbered in midfield by a 2-3-1.
Best for
Casual games and mixed-ability groups.
Use 2-2-2 in the lineup builder →

1-4-1

One defender, four midfielders and a striker.

Strengths
Floods the middle and wins the ball back quickly.
Watch out for
Needs a disciplined lone defender and runners from midfield.
Best for
Possession-based teams.

3-1-2

Three defenders, one holding midfielder and two strikers.

Strengths
Solid base plus two forwards to stretch the defence.
Watch out for
The lone midfielder covers a lot of ground.
Best for
Direct, counter-attacking teams.
Use 3-1-2 in the lineup builder →

2-1-3

Two defenders, one midfielder and a front three.

Strengths
Lots of width and shots in the final third.
Watch out for
Leaves the midfield thin.
Best for
When you need goals.
