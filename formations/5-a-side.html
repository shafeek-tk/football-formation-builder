5-a-side Formations Explained

Five-a-side is about movement more than shape: with a keeper and four outfield players, everyone attacks and everyone defends. These are the shapes most teams use, from the solid 2-2 to the all-out 1-3.

2-2

Two at the back, two up front. The simplest and most popular 5-a-side shape.

Strengths
Easy to understand, covers the pitch evenly, always two players back.
Watch out for
The middle can be empty, so midfield runners need to drop in.
Best for
New groups and casual weekly games.

2-1-1

A diamond: two defenders, one midfielder and a lone striker.

Strengths
Strong defensive base and a clear link player in the middle.
Watch out for
The striker can get isolated if the midfielder does not push up.
Best for
Teams with one confident finisher.

1-2-1

The classic diamond: one defender, two wide midfielders, one striker.

Strengths
Great width and passing angles, ideal for keeping the ball.
Watch out for
Only one natural defender, so the wide players must track back.
Best for
Fit, technical teams who like to pass.

1-1-2

One defender, one holding player and a front two.

Strengths
Two strikers keep the opposition busy and press high.
Watch out for
Exposed on the counter if the holder is beaten.
Best for
Teams chasing goals.

3-1

Three defenders and one striker.

Strengths
Very hard to break down; good for protecting a lead.
Watch out for
Few options going forward, the striker needs to hold the ball up.
Best for
Defending a lead or facing a stronger side.
Use 3-1 in the lineup builder →

1-3

One defender behind a front three.

Strengths
Maximum pressure high up the pitch and lots of shooting chances.
Watch out for
Very open at the back; the keeper becomes a sweeper.
Best for
Late in the game when you need a goal.

2-0-2

Two defenders and two forwards with no fixed midfield.

Strengths
Clear roles: two stay, two go.
Watch out for
The gap in the middle means lots of long balls.
Best for
Physical games and big pitches.
