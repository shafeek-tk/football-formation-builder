Best 9-a-Side Formations Explained

3-3-2 – The most popular 9v9 formation. Three defenders, three midfielders, and two strikers provides balance across the pitch. The midfield trio controls tempo while the front pair offers width.

3-2-3 – Three at the back, two in midfield, three forwards. An attacking formation that stretches the opposition with a front three. Requires the midfield pair to work hard covering the space.

4-3-1 – A defensive setup with four defenders, three midfielders, and a lone striker. Very difficult to break down. The striker holds the ball up for midfield runners to join the attack.

4-2-2 – Four defenders and two midfielders behind two strikers. A direct formation that's solid at the back and looks to get the ball forward quickly to the front pair.

3-4-1 – Three defenders, a dominant four-player midfield, and one striker. Controls possession through midfield numbers. The wide midfielders provide width and attacking support.

9-a-Side Football FAQ

What is the best 9v9 formation?
The 3-3-2 is the most balanced choice. It covers the pitch well and transitions smoothly between defence and attack. The 3-2-3 works if you want to play more attacking football.

What age group plays 9-a-side?
In England, 9v9 is the standard format for Under-12 (Year 7) football. It's the final step before full 11-a-side and helps players understand shape, width, and positional responsibility on a larger pitch.

What size pitch is used for 9v9?
A 9v9 pitch is typically 70-80m long and 45-55m wide — roughly three-quarters of a full-size pitch. This gives players more space to develop passing range and tactical awareness.
