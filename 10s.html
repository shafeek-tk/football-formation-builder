Best 10-a-Side Formations Explained

4-3-2 – The most popular 10v10 formation. Four defenders, three midfielders, two strikers. Almost identical structure to the classic 4-3-2-1 in 11-a-side but without the extra striker. Solid and balanced.

3-4-2 – Three at the back with a dominant four-player midfield and two forwards. The midfield four provides width and control, while three centre-backs give cover against counter-attacks.

4-2-3 – Four defenders, two holding midfielders, three forwards. An attacking formation that commits three players forward. The double pivot protects the back four while the front three cause havoc.

3-3-3 – A symmetrical formation with three players in each line. Simple to understand and provides even coverage across the pitch. Works well for casual games and pickup football.

10-a-Side Football FAQ

When would you play 10-a-side?
10v10 is common in casual and pickup football when you have enough players for nearly a full side. It's also used in some youth leagues as a transition format before full 11-a-side. Many Sunday league games end up 10v10 when someone doesn't turn up.

What's the difference between 10v10 and 11v11?
You simply remove one outfield player. This often means dropping a midfielder or forward, which opens up more space on the pitch. The game tends to be slightly more open and attacking as a result.
